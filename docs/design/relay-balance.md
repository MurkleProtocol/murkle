# Relay balance (as built, signet): users prepay, the operator never pays

Replaces the ticket design in `paid-relay.md`, which was rejected because it forced scheduled sweeps and random holds (a new buyer waited 3 to 8 hours). Here the only waits are the ones Bitcoin itself imposes (one confirmation of a deposit) and the ones a user chooses (hourly or 10-hour batch).

This design closes the ten findings of an internal adversarial review of its first draft (table in §7). None of the fixes adds a wait. Most are one rule each; the privacy ones are honest warnings in the wallet.

## 1. Rules

1. No free mode. The operator never pays any part of a user's transaction: not fee spikes, failed carriers, reorgs, rounding or bugs.
2. No forced random or scheduled waits. A relayed Fast or Next-block send goes out as soon as the balance covers it. Nothing is ever held for a top-up, a sweep or a timer.
3. Self-pay (the built-in key or Unisat pays the carrier) is always available and needs no relayer.
4. Simple enough for a normal user: top up, see a balance, send.

## 2. How it works

**Account.** The wallet derives a relay account key from the 24 words (label `murkle/relay-account/v1/<network>`, so signet and mainnet accounts differ). Account id = sha256 of its public key. Nothing is registered with the relayer before money arrives.

**Deposit addresses.** The relayer publishes one pool public key `Q`. Deposit address number `n` of an account is a P2TR output whose key is `Q + H(Q || id || n) * G`. Only the relayer can spend it. While the network is signet, any signet wallet can pay it, Unisat included: a plain payment, no OP_RETURN. Never send real bitcoin to it.
- The wallet shows address `n` until it receives a payment, then shows `n + 1`. Every top-up therefore goes to a fresh address, and an unpaid address is never skipped, so a restore only needs the relayer's `nextIndex` (below).
- Payments to an older address of the account are still credited (a wallet may keep it in its address book).

**Credit.** After the deposit has 1 confirmation (signet; 3 on mainnet), the wallet sends `POST /api/relay/credit {outpoint, accountPub, n}`. It needs no signature: it can only credit a deposit to the account it pays. The relayer:
1. parses the outpoint strictly: 64 lowercase hex characters, `:`, a decimal vout with no sign, spaces or leading zeros. Anything else is refused. The result is the one key for that output;
2. claims the key in memory before any network call (the same pattern `validateAndAccept` uses for nullifiers), and releases it if the credit fails;
3. checks with the explorer that the output pays deposit address `n` of this account, has the confirmations, and is at least the minimum deposit. It refuses an output of any transaction the relayer itself made;
4. in one durable write, records the key as credited and adds `value − sweepCost` to the balance, then replies.

A repeat call for a credited key returns the existing credit, so retries, two tabs and two devices are harmless. `sweepCost` is the cost of later spending that input at the cap rate, so the deposit pays for its own use. The wallet does all this automatically; the user just sees the balance appear.

**Send.** `POST /api/relay/submit {envelope, mode, accountPub, t, sig}`, optionally with the signed boolean `linkable`. Under one lock the relayer:
1. checks the signature, the pool cover (below: 409 `pool_thin` unless `linkable: true`) and the proof;
2. builds the exact carrier at the current fee rate;
3. computes `cost = fee + margin`;
4. debits the account (refusing with 402 `balance_low` if it can't cover);
5. records the debit durably;
6. signs and broadcasts.

Fast goes out immediately, Next block at the next block. If the fee rate is above the relayer's cap, the send is refused at once with `fee_high` and the wallet offers self-pay. It is never held.

**Batch sends.** At submit the relayer reserves 2x the cost from the balance. At release it charges the exact cost: first from the reservation, then from the free balance, and returns any difference.
- If an item still cannot be paid (balance short, or the rate is above the cap), it leaves its epoch: status `missed`, reservation returned. The rest of the epoch goes out on time. The wallet offers its usual choices: Retry in the next batch, Send at the next block, Pay the fee myself.
- A missed item is never sent later on its own, and a top-up never releases it. (A lone late carrier with an old batch anchor would stand out and tie the top-up to that transfer.)
- An epoch is held whole only when the relayer cannot send at all (fee rate unknown, too few pool coins), never because of one account.

**Once broadcast, the fee is spent.** If the carrier is later rejected (for example the user spent the same notes elsewhere first), the user has still paid for it. The relayer never bumps a carrier (an RBF or CPFP bump would evict or pay for other users' carriers chained on it). A user whose send is stuck taps "Pay the fee myself": the envelope does not depend on its carrier, so the user's own copy can land first. The wallet says that the relayed fee stays spent if the relayer's carrier is also mined.

**Pool coins.** The relayer never finds coins by listing an address. Its coin set is built only from its own records: credited deposits (from the credit records) and outputs of its journaled transactions. It checks them by outpoint and by the status of its own transactions. Coins anyone else sends to its keys, dust floods included, are invisible to it and never counted or spent. All change, from carriers, fan-outs and merges, goes to a separate pool change key `C`, never to a deposit address. `C` can be rotated without touching any deposit address. No carrier spends a deposit: deposits reach `C` through a merge paid from the margin (each deposit's sweep cost), and carriers pick at random among `C`'s coins (review fix, contract §4.5).

**Pool cover (privacy-trace-test.md L1).** The chain shows which deposits a pool coin's value came from, so a carrier funded from a coin that descends only from A's deposit is tied to A by its input, whatever the relayer does at random. So:
- The relayer records, per unspent pool coin, the distinct depositing accounts its value descends from, as opaque tags (an HMAC, under a key derived from the pool secret, of the account's opaque books key), never the account ids. A merge or carrier output descends from the accounts of all its inputs. A spent coin's tags are dropped once the transaction that spent it is in a block.
- A merge never takes a deposit alone. It takes the new deposits together with a confirmed pool coin of other accounts when there is one (the one adding the most accounts); with none, deposits of two or more accounts merge together; a lone account's deposits wait for company, unless a queued send has no other coin. The extra input is paid from the margin like the rest (I-PAY unchanged). Which coins a merge takes is decided by one deterministic plan, and the cover the relayer publishes before a merge counts only that plan's output, so it never claims cover the next merge cannot deliver.
- A carrier spends only a coin that descends from at least `k + 1` accounts (`k` = `MURKLE_RELAY_MIN_MIX`: 3 on signet, 5 on mainnet; 0 turns the rule off, and mainnet refuses 0). The rule is the same whoever sends: every sender then has at least `k` others in the coin's history, and the coin a carrier spends does not show whether its sender is in that history (timing still can, §5). (An earlier rule, "`k` accounts other than the sender", let a coin of exactly `k` accounts carry only the sends of accounts outside it, so its carrier named its sender by exclusion.) With no such coin the pool is **thin**, for every sender alike: submit answers 409 `pool_thin` ("Too few people have topped up the relay pool, so this send's input would tie it to your top-up address. Pay the fee yourself, or confirm to send it linkable."), with nothing reserved. Signed again with `linkable: true`, the send goes on and its carrier takes the coin with the widest history there is; the 202 says `linkable: true` and `thin: true`. A queued send that is not linkable and finds no cover when it is due becomes `missed` (`pool_thin`), nothing charged.
- `/api/relay/info` publishes `balance.mix = { k, coverOk, depositors }`: whether a coin descending from at least `k + 1` accounts can fund a carrier now (always false when `k` is 0), and how many accounts have had a deposit credited. Wallets treat a `k` below 3 as no cover.

**Balance.** `POST /api/relay/account {accountPub, t, sig}` returns `{balance, reserved, nextIndex}`. Shown in the wallet; never expires on signet. Withdrawal of the remainder to any address, with the fee paid from the balance, is stage 2.

**Signed requests.** `sig` is a BIP340 signature by the account key over `taggedHash("murkle/relay/v1", endpoint || network || Q || sha256(request body without sig))`. For submit the body is the envelope and the mode (and `linkable` when present, signed as JSON `true` / `false`), so a copied request cannot carry another envelope or change the mode or the consent, and nullifier dedup stops a replay of the same envelope. For the balance read, `t` must be within 10 minutes. The relayer never logs request bodies.

## 3. The invariant (the operator never pays)

I-PAY: every satoshi the relayer spends as a miner fee is covered by user money that was deposited, confirmed and credited to the account that caused the spend, before the signature is released.

- **I0, provenance.** The relayer only signs inputs that are:
  - credited deposit outputs (spendable through the account tweak), or
  - outputs of its own journaled transactions.

  Any other coin at its keys, an operator top-up included, is never spent. There is no "fund the relayer" command. Every output except the OP_RETURN pays `C`. The only exceptions are the emergency path of §9: an evacuation pays every controlled coin to the operator's cold address, and after a rotation the operator's refill (`refund-pool`, recorded as the relayer's own coin) restores exactly what the books owe.
- **I1, coverage.** One signing function computes the fee from the final transaction bytes and refuses unless the paying account's balance, already debited, covers it. The same function refuses any output to a deposit address, and checks in memory that the pool's recorded coins are at least balances + reservations + margin. Pool housekeeping (consolidations) is paid from the margin account, which only margins fill.
- **I2, books.** Every block: sum of credits over distinct credited outputs − sum of fees paid − sum of balances and reservations = margin ≥ 0, and the pool's recorded unspent coins ≥ balances + reservations + margin. On any mismatch the relayer stops (no new spends), while self-pay keeps working.

**I-PAY, extended for mining claims** (`mining.md` §9, `mining-contract.md` §9.6). A relayed mining claim's carrier also pays the token's service-fee outputs (on signet, 500 sats to the Murkle platform address). Every satoshi that leaves the pool, as a miner fee **or as a service-fee output**, is covered by a recorded debit of the account that caused it before the signature is released. The relayer pays a fee recipient from pool coins only against such a debit:
- **I0** for claims: every output except the OP_RETURN pays `C`, **or** is one of the service-fee outputs that the relayer's own indexer requires for that envelope's token (`requiredFeeOutputs`), with exactly that script and amount, in that order after the OP_RETURN (output 0), the change to `C` at a random position among them (L5). They are derived from the envelope and the indexer's terms, never from the request. A token whose fee output would pay `C`, the pool key or a deposit address is refused (`mine_unsupported`) before any debit. Outputs to deposit addresses stay refused.
- **I1** for claims: `signPoolTx` computes `spent = Σ inputs − Σ outputs to C` from the final transaction and refuses unless `spent` equals the miner fee plus the service-fee outputs. It then charges `cost = fee + service + margin` (the margin is on the miner fee only) and saves the charge before signing.
- **I2** for claims: `credited − fees − serviceOut − Σ balance − Σ reserved = margin ≥ 0`, with `serviceOut` a new book total (written to the books file only once it is non-zero). A carrier refunded because it never reached the network reverses its `serviceOut` with its fee and margin; a carrier that reached the network and then vanished while its input came back moves its fee and its service fee to the margin.
- Once broadcast, both the miner fee and the service fee are spent, even if the claim later loses at the supply cap or expires. The relayer never refunds from operator money and never accelerates a carrier: a claim's carrier pays the next-block rate with headroom up front, spends a confirmed coin only, and its change is not spent until it confirms (the fee recipient could otherwise hang a chain of descendants on its output, `mining.md` §8.8).

## 4. Spam

Every carrier is debited from a balance that real BTC filled. A zero-value transfer costs the spammer the same as a real one. With no balance, a request is refused before any proof check. Invalid proofs from an account cost a small fee (rate-limited per account). On signet, faucet coins are free, so spam costs the spammer only faucet time; the operator still loses nothing.

## 5. Privacy, stated honestly

- **Chain observers.**
  - They see address A pay a fresh deposit address. Once the relayer spends that coin, anyone can tell it was a Murkle relay deposit. Separate top-ups go to separate addresses, so they are not linked to each other by the address.
  - Carriers are funded from pool coins, never from A's own deposit. That alone did not hide A: while only A had money in the pool, every carrier descended from A's deposit on chain (the signet trace test named the sender that way). Now every carrier spends only a coin that descends from at least `k + 1` depositors (`k` is 3 on signet, 5 on mainnet), whoever sends, so its input points at a group of at least `k + 1` top-up addresses. A's may or may not be one of them; if it is, at least `k` others are too. While fewer people have topped up, the relayer refuses the send (`pool_thin`) unless A confirms it as linkable, and then its input can tie it to A's top-up address.
  - **Timing is the weak spot.** While few people use the relayer, a send made right after a deposit confirms is very likely to be linked to the address that paid it, because almost no other relayed carriers go out in those blocks. Topping up ahead of time, or using a batch mode, hides this better, but a batch hides nothing while it holds only your transfer, and the proof's anchor fixes how many notes were in the tree (privacy-trace-test.md L3). No wait is added for it; the wallet warns instead (below).
- **The operator** knows which account sent which relayed transfer, and the IP and time, but not the contents (amount, token, recipient stay hidden by the proof). It also learns the BTC address you topped up from, because a deposit's inputs are public. So the relayer can link that address to every transfer you relay with this balance. Tor does not prevent this, because the account is the same on every connection.
- **What the relayer keeps on disk:** per-account balances, reservations and deposit-address counters, each under an opaque key (an HMAC of the account id under a key derived from the pool secret), never the account id; the credited deposits; the global sums for the books; the unspent pool coins with their opaque lineage tags (a deposit coin with its tweak, not its account); and the carrier ledger as published at `/api/relay/ledger` (txid, fee, size, rate), with no account field. An item carries its account key, cost, submit height and mode only until it is broadcast or its reservation is settled; then the saved item keeps only its status answer (and, while its carrier may still be re-sent, what its own raw bytes show). A credited deposit keeps its account key and amount only while the pool still holds it or wallets still warn about it as a recent top-up (until 5 relayed transfers land after it, at most 144 blocks); then it keeps only its value, so the files no longer tell whose it was. The retired v1 file is cut down the same way at startup: no envelope, nullifier or charge of any old item stays.
  - Why: each carrier's charge is a fixed function of its public fee (fee plus margin, plus a claim's service outputs), so dropping the saved charge alone hid nothing. Before this fix, credits by account minus the balance gave each account's total spend, and matching that to the public fees named the carriers it paid for (the trace test's insider did it, and so did the review on the first fix). Now the files give no account's credits once they are settled, and no account id from which its deposit addresses could be found on chain.
  - What a leak of the files still shows: each account's balance and how many deposit addresses it has used, and the credits still in the pool or recent, with their account keys. An account whose credits are all recent can then be matched to its carriers exactly, as before. With only a handful of accounts and sends, the balances, the public deposit values and the public fees can still be fitted together to name who sent what; the more people relay, the more ways the numbers fit. The operator itself always knows (above).
- **Self-pay** ties the paying address to the transfer on chain.

**In the wallet:**
- Top-up screen: "Top up once, it lasts many sends." The suggested amount covers about 10 sends, not the minimum. Next to the balance: "Relayed transfers in the last 144 blocks: N".
- Send screen, while fewer than 5 relayed transfers have landed since your last deposit confirmed: "Your top-up confirmed recently and few people are relaying right now. Sending now can link this transfer to the address you paid from. A batch mode, or waiting, hides this better." The user can still send at once.
- Relay settings: "The relayer can link the address you top up from to every transfer you relay with this balance. Tor does not prevent this. It cannot see amounts, tokens or recipients."

**Mining claims are different** (`mining.md` §9). The sentence "It cannot see amounts, tokens or recipients" is true for transfers only and is never shown for mining. A claim's token and reward are public on chain, and the relayer learns, per account: which token, how many claims and their reward, the top-up address, the IP and the time. Waiting does not help: a solution must land within 12 blocks, so batch modes are refused (`mine_mode`). The wallet's copy on the relay route is: "The reward goes to a private note. Chain observers see relay claims of TICKER for R each, not who received them. The relayer can link the address you top up from to every claim it carries for you, including the token and the reward. While few people relay claims, the claims right after your top-up are easy to tie to it. Top up before you start mining." The send-screen warning above counts relayed claims next to relayed transfers.

Optional later: anonymous spending of a balance with blind tokens, issued instantly (no sweeps, no holds). The operator then sees only "account X converted N tokens at time t". Also later: more than one relay account per seed, to keep identities apart.

## 6. Parameters (signet defaults)

1. Margin: 10% plus a 50-sat floor per carrier (signet).
2. Deposit confirmations: 1 on signet, 3 on mainnet.
3. Minimum deposit: 2,000 sats (below that: not credited, stated before paying).
4. Batch reservation headroom: 2x the fee at submit, exact fee charged at release; a short item becomes missed.
5. Above the fee cap, Fast and Next-block are refused (self-pay offered) instead of held. Batch items above the cap at release become missed.
6. Suggested top-up of about 10 sends.
6b. Pool cover `k` (`MURKLE_RELAY_MIN_MIX`): 3 on signet, 5 on mainnet, 0..32; a carrier's coin descends from at least `k + 1` accounts; 0 turns the rule off (refused on mainnet; wallets treat any `k` below 3 as no cover).
7. No relayer bumps; a stuck send is fixed with "Pay the fee myself".
8. Mining claims (once mining is active): `block` or `fast` only; the carrier rate is the next-block rate x 1.25 (`MURKLE_RELAY_MINE_FEE_HEADROOM`), quoted at 684 vB; refused work costs 20 sats (`MURKLE_RELAY_INVALID_POW_SATS`) and counts toward the invalid limit; at most 64 Argon2 tasks queued (`MURKLE_RELAY_POW_QUEUE`) and one work check per account at a time; a claim is signed only while the tip is at most `ref + 9` and re-sent only while it is at most `ref + 11`. A relayed claim has no "Pay the fee myself" fallback while it is pending: paying it yourself would publish the same solution from your address.

## 7. Adversarial review (10 findings)

| # | Finding | Resolution |
|---|------|------------|
| 1 | One deposit credited many times (parallel calls, spelling variants, restart) | Strict outpoint parsing to one key, claimed in memory before any await, credited and saved in one durable write; idempotent repeat; I2 sums distinct outputs; pool check before every signature (§2 Credit, §3). |
| 2 | A user's bump evicts other users' carriers chained on it | No relayer bumps. Stuck sends use "Pay the fee myself" with the same envelope (§2). |
| 3 | Dust flood at the pool's address breaks UTXO listing and stalls the relayer | Coins tracked by outpoint from credit records and the journal; no address is ever listed; foreign coins ignored; separate change key `C` (§2 Pool coins). |
| 4 | One account's shortfall holds a whole batch epoch | A short item leaves its epoch as missed with its reservation returned; the rest goes out on time; an epoch is never held for one account (§2 Batch sends). |
| 5 | Request signature not tied to the request | BIP340 signature over a tag, endpoint, network, `Q` and the body hash; credit needs none; bodies never logged (§2 Signed requests). |
| 6 | Send right after a deposit confirms is a timing link | Cannot be removed at low volume without a wait, so it is disclosed: send-screen warning, top-up-ahead copy, meter next to the balance (§5). |
| 7 | One fixed deposit address per seed links all top-up sources | Fresh deposit address per top-up (index `n`); old ones still credited (§2 Deposit addresses). |
| 8 | Operator can map the depositor's address to every relayed transfer | Disclosed in §5 and in the wallet. Nothing on disk links an account to a carrier once it is settled: since the 2026-10-07 fix (trace test L2) that includes the per-item cost, submit height and mode, which before let credits − costs = balance re-attribute carriers. Blind tokens remain the later full fix. |
| 9 | A batch item held for a shortfall goes out alone after the top-up | Never sent outside its epoch's release; a top-up does not release it; it becomes missed (§2 Batch sends). |
| 10 | Carrier change could go back to a deposit address (and be credited again) | All change pays `C`; the signing function refuses outputs to deposit addresses; credit refuses outputs of the relayer's own transactions (§2, §3 I0/I1). |

## 9. Compromised keys: evacuation and rotation

The pool key `Q` (every deposit address is a tweak of it) and the change key `C` hold every user's prepaid money. If either may be known to someone else, the operator moves the money away first and asks questions later. The playbook is `docs/OPERATIONS.md` 10.4.1.

**Rules.**
1. No user loses balance because of an evacuation or a rotation. The books keep every balance and reservation as they were; queued sends are released (missed, `relayer_evacuating`, reservation returned, nothing charged); carriers already broadcast keep their status and their charge.
2. The evacuation is the operator's cost, never a user's (I-PAY unchanged): its fees are paid from the margin account first; what the margin cannot pay is recorded as the operator's liability, `operator = { in, owed }` in the books. I2 becomes `credited + operator.in − fees − serviceOut − Σ balance − Σ reserved = margin ≥ 0`; `owed` drops to 0 when the operator refills the pool. A sweep that never confirms, or whose cheaper version confirmed, gives its fee back (the liability first).
3. Only the relayer's own coins move, found as always from its records (I0): credited deposits not merged yet (spent through their tweaks of `Q`) and change of its own journaled transactions. No address is listed; a coin anyone else sent to `Q`, `C` or a deposit address is never touched.
4. A key is never used twice: the rotation refuses a new key whose x-only public key this relayer (any generation) or the retired v1 relayer ever used.
5. Balances belong to account keys, not to a pool key. The books store accounts under `HMAC(HMAC(pool secret, label), account id)`; at the first rotation those two HMAC keys (books and lineage) are written to `account-tags.key` and stay the same in every later generation, so every account finds its balance under the new pool key. (Whoever holds an old pool secret can derive them, as before the rotation: with a copy of the files they can match a known account id to its balance, and never spend anything.)

**Freezing a running relayer.** The operator tools write the relayer's files, so the running relayer must stop writing first. Each tool places `HOLD` (JSON `{ reason, nonce }`) in the relay directory; the relayer looks for it every second and at every request and tick. Once seen, new sends and credits are refused at once; under its lock (after any flush in progress) the relayer releases its queued items when the reason is `evacuate`, saves once, sets itself frozen (no write, no tick, no signature) and writes `HOLD.ack`. A relayer started while `HOLD` is in place starts frozen. A tool goes on only once the running relayer has acknowledged its `HOLD` (`HOLD.ack` with the same nonce), or when the operator says the service is stopped (`--relayer-stopped`); otherwise it refuses and leaves `HOLD` in place. Every write of a tool first checks that the state file is still the one it read, and while `HOLD` is in place the relayer writes only its freeze's one save, and only if the state file is still the one it last wrote, so neither side ever overwrites the other. Balance reads are still answered.

**Evacuation** (`murkle relayer evacuate --to <cold address>`). Journaled carriers are settled first, by the relayer's own rules: one the explorer knows is broadcast (its charge final); any other is sent again (it is a user's paid transaction, signed before the `HOLD`): accepted, it is broadcast and its change is swept; refused with its input gone, it is dropped and nothing is charged; one whose earlier broadcast answer was lost stays journaled and charged (I-PAY: never refunded while it may land) until it is known or its anchor window closes, and the rotation waits for it. Past its window and unknown to the network it can no longer count: it keeps its charge, as in normal operation, and its input, still under the old key, comes back and is swept (its fee goes to the margin). Merges and fan-outs are sent again; one the network refuses is dropped (its fee back to the margin). Sweeps of an earlier run are settled before anything is re-sent: a version in a block confirms its sweep (after a bump whose own broadcast failed, the earlier version that won counts, and only its fee), and a sweep refused for a missing input is kept when any of its versions is known to the network. Then every controlled coin is swept to the cold address: confirmed coins in transactions of at most 1,500 inputs; unconfirmed coins (whose parents the explorer knows) apart, with at most 24 unconfirmed ancestors per transaction. Each sweep has one output, inputs in a random order, every input RBF (`0xfffffffd`), the one fee rule, at `--fee-rate` or twice the higher of the next-block rate and the relayer's rate (at least 2 sat/vB): the thief holds the same key, so the sweep must outbid them. Every sweep is journaled (in the state and in the public ledger as kind `evacuation`) before the first broadcast. A sweep refused because a conflicting transaction is in the mempool is reported `contested` (bump it); one refused for a missing input is split into one sweep per input, and an input still missing is recorded lost. `--bump [--fee-rate n]` signs every unconfirmed sweep again with the same coins at a higher whole rate; the difference is booked like the first fee. Fees above half of the value a run sweeps are refused unless `--high-fee` (a mistyped rate must not burn the pool). `--dry-run` writes, holds and broadcasts nothing. `--bump` and `--cancel` first check, without a `HOLD`, that there is a sweep to bump or an evacuation to cancel. `--cancel` undoes a false alarm only while no sweep has moved a coin (none journaled, or every one dropped): the evacuation record goes and the `HOLD` is lifted.

While evacuating, `/api/relay/info` answers `code: "relayer_evacuating"` and `balance.depositsOpen: false`; submit and credit answer 503 `relayer_evacuating`; `/api/health` reports the relayer halted with that code.

**Rotation** (`murkle relayer rotate`), once every sweep has confirmed (or with `--accept-lost` for coins a thief took). New `Q` and `C`; the books carried over unchanged (`RelayBooks.rekeyed`); every item and the ledger carried over; every late deposit (below) whose sweep into the old pool has confirmed is credited first, since its coin left with the evacuation and the refill covers it (a sweep of late deposits still unconfirmed holds the rotation, like the evacuation's own); no coin (the pool is empty); the old keys and the last old state kept in `retired/g<N>/` (written durably, owner-only, like `account-tags.key`); the state records `generation`, `retired: [{ generation, pool, change }]` (public keys) and `rotation: { funded: false }`. The files are written next to the old ones and renamed into place (a re-run finishes an interrupted rotation). Until the pool is refilled the relayer refuses every send and credit with 503 `pool_unfunded`, while `/api/relay/info` already publishes the new `poolKey`, `balance.generation` and `balance.retiredPoolKeys`.

**Refill** (`murkle relayer refund-pool --from <cold key file>`, or `--outpoint <txid:vout>` for a payment made by hand). The operator pays the shortfall from the cold wallet to the new `C`: balances + reservations + margin + late deposits swept but not credited yet, less the pool's coins in a block and what those deposits' unconfirmed sweeps bring. The payment is recorded as a pool coin (I0: the relayer's own record, public in the ledger as kind `refund` with fee 0) with the lineage of the evacuated coins (pay it from the sweep's output); an output of the relayer's own records is refused. A held coin (a refill, a sweep of late deposits) counts in I2's `poolUnspent` only once it is in a block and is not spent before then: a refill that is replaced or evicted never makes I2 hold. So the refill is recorded at once and the command is run again with the same `--outpoint` after its block; a second `--from` while one waits is refused. When the pool covers the books, `owed` is paid, `rotation.funded` is set and `HOLD` is removed; the relayer resumes at its next restart. After the first refill the same command pays any later operator liability (below), under a maintenance `HOLD` of its own. This is the only way operator money enters the pool, and it only restores what the books already owe.

**Late deposits.** A credit request for an output paying address `n` of an account under a retired `Q` is checked as any deposit (output, minimum, confirmations), then recorded (opaque account key, its tweak under the old key, the value) and answered 409 `deposit_retired`; a repeat gets the same answer, another account `already_credited`. Nothing is credited from a key a thief may hold. `murkle relayer sweep-retired` (under a maintenance `HOLD`) sweeps the recorded deposits with the retired key into the new `C` (RBF, high rate, fee as an operator cost, ledger kind `retired-sweep`, output not spent before it confirms); once it confirms, each deposit is credited `value − sweepCost` like any deposit, provided the pool covers it: a credit adds `value` to the liabilities while the pool gained `value − fee`, so when the margin could not pay the whole fee (the rest is an operator liability), the deposit waits (`deposit_retired`, status `swept`) until `refund-pool` has covered it. The relayer never halts for it and every other send goes on. One a thief spent first is recorded lost and not credited. During an evacuation `sweep-retired` settles, re-sends and bumps its earlier sweeps but starts no new one (`C` may be compromised too).

**Wallets.** Deposit addresses always come from the current `poolKey`. While `balance.depositsOpen` is false (or the code is `relayer_evacuating`, `pool_unfunded` or `maintenance`) neither wallet shows or pays any deposit address; the balance is still read. With `balance.retiredPoolKeys` non-empty both say that every earlier address is retired and must never be paid again, and that the balance carried over. The web wallet keeps its per-pool-key records (`prefs.relayBy`) and asks the relayer to credit a payment still pending under a retired key (`deposit_retired` keeps it waiting; a credit removes it); the CLI keeps such a payment in `relay.pending`. Since a payment to an old address can come from anywhere (an exchange, another wallet), both also look, on each deposit check, at the last address numbers they showed under each retired key (the web wallet from that key's record, the CLI below its current number; every number with `older` / `--older`) and ask for any payment found there.

**Tests** (`test/relayer-evacuate.test.mjs`, FakeEsplora and temporary state only): the hold freezes a running relayer; every controlled coin and nothing foreign is swept; the fee comes from the margin and then as an operator liability, balances unchanged; dry run; bump; the rotation never reuses a key (old pool, old change, a negation, the v1 key), carries the books over, and I2 fails until the refill and holds after; refill by key and by outpoint; late deposits recorded, swept and credited; the CLI and web wallets on the new key, finding payments made from elsewhere to old addresses. From the review: a late deposit whose sweep fee the margin cannot pay never halts the relayer and is credited after the refill (also when its sweep was unconfirmed at refill time); a second evacuation and rotation credit a swept late deposit; a carrier whose answer was lost holds the rotation until its window closes and its coin is then swept; a journaled carrier is sent, not refunded; an earlier sweep version that wins after a failed bump is settled, nothing lost; an unconfirmed refill never funds the pool; a stray `--bump` or `--cancel` changes nothing and `--cancel` undoes a false alarm; no acknowledgement, no evacuation, and a running relayer never overwrites a tool's write; the fee guard; own outputs and retired addresses refused as refill and cold address.
